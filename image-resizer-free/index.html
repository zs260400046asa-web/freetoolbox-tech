Image Resizer Free: Resize Any Photo in Your Browser

An image resizer changes the width and height of a photo so it fits exactly where it has to go — a listing, a profile picture, a slide or a post. Doing it free in your browser takes about ten seconds: open the image, pick the size, download it. No signup, no install, no watermark.

Resize any image in 3 steps

  1. Open your image. Drag it onto the Image Resizer or choose a file — JPG, PNG and WebP all work. Nothing uploads anywhere: the image stays on your device and the resizing runs in your browser.
  2. Pick the size. Type the width or the height in pixels, or shrink the image by percent. Keep the lock on so the width and height stay in proportion — that is what stops the photo looking stretched or squashed.
  3. Download it. Save the resized copy and check the dimensions before you upload it. The original file on your device stays untouched, so you can always go back and resize it again.

The sizes people actually need

Most resize jobs are for one of these. Start from the biggest original you have and shrink down to the size you need — shrinking keeps quality, stretching a small photo does not.

Resize from big to small, never small to big. A 4000 pixel photo shrinks to 1080 cleanly. A 300 pixel photo stretched to 1080 turns soft and blurry, and no resizer can put the missing detail back. If the photo is already too small, enlarge it first with the Image Upscaler and then resize it down.

Resizer or compressor — which one do you need

People mix these two up because both make a file smaller. They do different jobs, and most uploads need both in this order.

  • Resizer changes the dimensions. A 4000 by 3000 photo becomes 1080 by 810. Use it when a site asks for an exact size or the photo is far bigger than the screen will ever show.
  • Compressor changes the file size. The dimensions stay the same and the megabytes shrink. Use it when the photo is already the right size but the file is too heavy to upload or it slows the page down. See the Image Compressor.
  • Use both together. Resize first to the size you need, then compress the result. A listing photo resized to 2000 pixels and then compressed loads fast and still looks sharp.
  • Transparency survives both. A transparent PNG stays transparent through the Resizer and the Compressor, as long as you download it as PNG again.

Questions

Is resizing an image really free?

Yes. The Image Resizer runs free in your browser, with no account and no watermark. Your image never leaves your device.

Will resizing make my photo blurry?

Shrinking a photo keeps it sharp. Enlarging a small photo is what makes it blurry, so always start from the biggest original you have.

Can I resize by percent instead of pixels?

Yes — pick 50 percent to halve the photo or 25 percent to quarter it. Percent is the fastest way to shrink a whole batch of photos to a similar size.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes, it runs in the phone browser the same way. Open the Resizer, choose the photo from your gallery, pick the size and download the result.

What if I need the photo bigger, not smaller?

Use the Image Upscaler to enlarge it first, then resize it to the exact dimensions you need. For grainy photos, see the guide on editing photos online.
