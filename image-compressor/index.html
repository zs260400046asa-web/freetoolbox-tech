Why compress your images?

Large images slow down websites, eat storage and get rejected by marketplaces with upload limits. Compression removes data your eyes cannot see, so a 4 MB camera photo can become a 400 KB image that looks the same on screen — pages load faster, listings upload without errors, and emails stop bouncing for size.

Sellers use it before uploading product photos, bloggers use it to speed up their pages, and everyone uses it when a form says "file too large".

Does compression reduce image quality?

At 80% quality, JPEG and WebP compression is visually lossless for most photos — the file gets much smaller while the picture looks the same. Very low quality settings (under 50%) can introduce visible blur and blockiness, which is why the slider shows you the result size before you download.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The compression happens entirely in your browser using your own device, so your images are never sent anywhere. You can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it will still work.

Which format should I choose?

JPEG is the safest choice for photos and works everywhere. WebP is usually 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same quality and is supported by all modern browsers and most marketplaces. Keep PNG only when you need a transparent background — PNG photos compress poorly.

Why does it say “already optimal — no savings”?

Some images — especially PNG screenshots and graphics that were already saved efficiently — cannot get smaller in the same format. Re-saving them can even make the file larger, so this tool keeps your original file as the download instead of handing you a bigger one. For PNGs, switching the output format to WebP usually does shrink the file a lot, so the tool suggests that when it happens.

Is there a file size or count limit?

No. Because everything runs on your device, you can compress as many images as you like, of any size your browser can open.
