Make Logo Transparent: Free, in Under a Minute

Someone sends you your own logo as a JPG on a white square, and now it looks wrong everywhere you put it. Here's how to free it from the box — free, in under a minute — and the one trap that makes transparent logos look cheap.

The quick method (3 steps)

  1. Take the largest, sharpest version of the logo you have — a small, blurry source gives a fuzzy cutout.
  2. Run it through the Background Remover at the top of this page. Logos cut out especially cleanly because their edges are sharp and their backgrounds are usually one flat colour.
  3. Leave the background set to transparent and download the PNG. That's your master logo file — keep it safe and reuse it everywhere.

Why it has to be a PNG

Transparency needs an alpha channel, and JPG simply doesn't have one — every JPG pixel is fully solid. PNG (and WebP) can store “see-through” per pixel. The full comparison, including when WebP beats PNG, is in the Blog.

The fuzzy-edge trap

If the original logo sat on white and was saved as a low-quality JPG, its edges carry a faint white-grey fringe from compression. On a white page you'll never see it; on a dark website header it glows. Fixes, in order: get a better source file (ask your designer for the original PNG or SVG file), re-run and check at 100% zoom, or place the logo on backgrounds close to white until you get a clean source. A vector file (SVG) from your designer beats any cutout — a remover is for when that file doesn't exist or is lost. Rough edges on photos too? See the Blog.

Make two versions: light and dark

A logo designed in dark colours with transparency will nearly disappear on a dark background. So keep two masters — your normal logo for light backgrounds, and a white or light version for dark ones. You can test both instantly here: after downloading the transparent PNG, preview your subject on the black and white swatches in the tool panel before you commit.

Where a transparent logo earns its keep

  • Website header & footer — sits on any colour scheme, light or dark.
  • Invoices, letterheads and documents — no white box clashing with the page.
  • Social posts & watermarks — drop it in a corner of product photos at reduced opacity.
  • Presentations — looks designed instead of pasted.

Questions

Can I make a logo transparent from a JPG?

Yes — that's exactly the case above. The JPG itself can't hold transparency, but removing its background and exporting as PNG gives you a transparent file.

Will the transparent logo lose quality?

The cutout keeps the resolution of your source file. Start from the largest version you have; a tiny, compressed JPG will stay soft no matter what tool you use.

Is an SVG better than a transparent PNG?

For logos, yes — a vector SVG scales to any size without softening. If your designer can send the SVG, use it. The PNG from this guide is the right answer when all you have is a flattened image.
